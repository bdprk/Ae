Measured effect (within-run old/new comparison, wall clock, 7 alternating runs)
  200 declarations: old 64.1 -> new 3.4 ms, saved 60.7 ms, ratio 18.80
  800 declarations: old 987.2 -> new 9.3 ms, saved 978.0 ms, ratio 106.73
  Both binaries emit byte-identical C (20,352 bytes, raw AND normalised) and
  byte-identical declaration-site diagnostics; the assertion is in the script.
  Four scenarios (ms): cold 38.2 | unchanged 5.4 | impl 40.0 | iface 40.2
  -O0 executable 17,168 bytes; no cache kind or generated C change.

Boundaries (do not overstate)
  - The speedup applies to files with MANY generic declarations. A program
    with one or two generic functions is within measurement noise; this is not
    a general "compiler is N times faster" claim.
  - One 800-decl baseline sample reached 1,629 ms under load; absolute numbers
    must not be compared across sessions or archives. Only the within-run
    old/new comparison is causal.
  - No peak-memory sampling was done; allocation counts drop but no specific
    memory figure is claimed.
  - No safety check was disabled, no cache kind or resident process added,
    and no translation unit was split (W3 stage 2 remains blocked by ADR-38).
